While youwait.

The order matters. Every minute the supply stays on is more water in the house.

  1. Shut off the main

    Close the main water supply to the building.

  2. Catch the leak

    Put a bucket under whatever is still dripping.

  3. Contain it

    Pack towels around the base so the water stays put.

  4. Call

    (226) 240-4250, any hour.

What counts asan emergency.

Pipes

  • Burst water pipes
  • Frozen lines, before they burst
  • Water coming through a ceiling

Drains

  • Basement drains backing up
  • Overflowing toilets
  • Storm drains overflowing in heavy rain

Pumps

  • A failed sump pump
  • A sump pump with no battery backup

Cold snaps andlong rain.

A frozen line.
Warm the pipe gently and keep the heat at a distance. Call even if the water starts flowing again, because the pipe may already be damaged.
Going away in winter.
Take precautions before you leave and give the emergency number to whoever is watching the house.
Days of rain.
Check the storm drains. If they look stressed, call before the water finds its way indoors.
After hours.
Emergency calls outside regular hours are billed at a higher rate. You are told the figure before the work begins.
