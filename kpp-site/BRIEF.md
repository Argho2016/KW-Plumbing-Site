# BRIEF: Kitchener Plumbing Pros, concept redesign

**Self-authored under explicit creative delegation.** The owner of this build asked for
a demo site "in their branding" that "still appeals to the target avatar" and feels
"way more elevated and way more premium", built from the real images and branding on
kwplumbingpros.com. No interview answers were given beyond that, so the eight topics
below are authored decisions, each marked as evidence or assumption.

## Evidence (inspected on the live site, 2026-11-24)

- Logo: "KPP" drawn as pipework (royal blue letters, red couplings, sitting on a cyan
  pipe) beside a light humanist wordmark "KITCHENER PLUMBING PROS."
- Colours measured from computed styles: royal blue `#213E9A`, red `#ED1B24`,
  cyan `#00ADEF`, white ground, grey body text. Face: Roboto.
- Four photographs (hero under-sink scene, macro sink drain with running water, plumber
  holding a chrome faucet, wrench on a chrome trap) plus flat service icons.
- Offer: residential, commercial and 24-hour emergency plumbing; drain and sewer
  cleaning (hydro-jetting, camera inspection, rooter), water heaters (tank and
  tankless), bathroom, kitchen, basement/laundry, outdoor.
- Claims the site makes: locally owned, master certified, licensed and insured,
  upfront estimates, clean-up after the job, quality guaranteed, on call weekends and
  holidays. Service area: Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge.
- Contact: (226) 240-4250, info@kwplumbingpros.com, 1201-812 King St W, Kitchener ON
  N2G 1E9. Existing CTA label: "Book A Service Call".
- Proof: ten Google reviews. Star rating not shown in the page text, so none is used.

## Assumptions

- The visitor is a homeowner or small-business owner in Kitchener-Waterloo, usually
  with something leaking, blocked or cold right now, often on a phone.
- No verified figures exist (response times, years in business, job counts), so the
  page carries no counters and no invented numbers.

## The eight topics (authored)

1. **Vibe:** porcelain, chrome, running water, calm competence. References: a
   well-lit showroom bathroom; the inside of a tidy van; a hotel service card.
2. **Journey, in order:** who they are and that they answer, name your problem, hear
   the price first, how a visit goes, the 3 a.m. emergency, what neighbours called
   about, book.
3. **Energy:** calm and bright almost everywhere. One intense act: the night
   emergency. Calm again to finish.
4. **Feeling and the one moment:** see the curve below. The moment: the page goes
   dark at 3 a.m., the pipe beside you springs a leak, and it is sealed the instant
   the phone number appears.
5. **The thing no other site does:** the logo's pipe leaves the logo and runs down
   the whole page as the navigation; water follows your scroll through it.
6. **Range:** premium, but high-key and warm in voice rather than dark-minimal. A
   plumber should feel clean and bright, not like a watch brand.
7. **World or scenes:** distinct scenes on one physical line (the pipe).
8. **Assets:** the site's own logo and four photographs. Nothing generated.

## Step 1

- **What and for whom:** a licensed local plumbing company for homes and businesses
  in Kitchener-Waterloo and Cambridge.
- **Belief to install:** these people will turn up, tell me the price before they
  start, and leave the place spotless.
- **One action, one label:** "Book a service call". The phone number is always
  visible as a number.
- **Art direction:** high-key editorial (worlds.md 2), built from supplied
  photography; one low-key act for the emergency.

## Journey

1. Arrival: a competent person is already under the sink
2. Naming: the visitor says what is wrong and sees it is ordinary work here
3. Price: the figure comes before the wrench
4. Visit: what actually happens when they come
5. Emergency: 3 a.m., and somebody answers
6. Neighbours: ten real jobs
7. Booking: one form, one number

## Feeling curve

1. Reassurance: a bright, tidy under-sink scene opens from a window to the whole
   screen, hands already on the wrench
2. Control: the visitor picks their own problem and the page answers in plain words
3. Trust: a wrench closes on chrome as the line "the price comes first" lands
4. Calm: four short promises on white, nothing moving (authored quiet before the peak)
5. Dread, then relief: hard cut to night, running water fills the screen, the pipe
   in the margin leaks; "We pick up." and the number arrive and the leak seals
6. Belonging: ten neighbours' jobs slide past sideways, each one labelled plainly
7. Resolve: the pipe turns the corner into the booking form and stops

## Peak

Act 5. "The whole page went dark, it said 3 a.m., the pipe on the side started
dripping, and it stopped the moment the phone number came up."

## Tell-someone sentence

It's the site where a pipe runs down the side of the page, springs a leak at
3 a.m., and is fixed by the time you reach the phone number.

## Authored silence

Act 4 is deliberately still: no pinning, no media, short. It is the quiet in front
of the peak, not dead scroll.

## Grammar, signature move, gate

- **Grammar: Service line (new).** One pipe is the page's spine and its navigation.
  Navigation: couplings on the pipe are the jump links; there is no top bar, only
  a logo plate and a phone pill. Sequence: sections are fixtures on the line, each
  on its own painted ground (no drift). Ending: the line turns into the booking
  form and terminates. Bans: no scrub video, no spotlight, no drift, no centred
  hero copy, no second navigation.
- **Why the eight lost:** filmic one-shot needs scrub footage and forbids visible
  sequence, and this audience needs to jump straight to the number; chaptered
  editorial forbids media above the fold; live surface and continuous world do not
  describe a trades business; typographic poster would waste the photographs;
  gallery forbids a claim; split stage has no two-sided argument; a cutlist is the
  wrong pulse for someone anxious about a leak.
- **Signature move:** the service line. Fill tracks scroll, couplings navigate, the
  line stops at a leak during the emergency act and catches up once it is sealed.
- **Fingerprint gate:** registry empty. First build, nothing to clear.

## Score

| Beat | Device | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | `pin` + `parallax` planes (window opens to full bleed) | outside becomes inside |
| Naming | `flow` + `in`, operable selector | agency, in the visitor's words |
| Price | `reveal` | a wipe is a change of state: unknown to known |
| Visit | `flow` + `in` | quiet, typographic |
| Emergency (peak) | `pin` + `kinetic`, span 3.8 | held breath, largest span |
| Neighbours | `pan` | lateral reads as breadth |
| Booking | `flow` | resolves and holds |

Seven acts, about 12.5 viewport-heights.

## Revision 2 (owner request: no white ground, more premium colour and motion, full site, backend)

- **Ground:** deep-water navy drawn from the logo's royal blue, ivory ink, the
  logo's cyan as the single accent, red kept for couplings and the emergency.
  The photographs stay bright, so each one reads as a lit window in a dark room.
- **Peak:** the page is dark throughout now, so the emergency act no longer wins
  by being the only dark one. It wins by being the only act where the whole
  screen moves: full-bleed running water, the leak in the service line, the
  largest span. Verified on the contact sheet as the largest visual change.
- **Motion added:** headings rise line by line, the hero photo and inset arrive
  on load, a magnetic primary button with a single band of light, pointer light
  on raised surfaces, water running in the service line, a menu that opens from
  the button, cross-page transitions. All of it is off under reduced motion.
- **Navigation:** a full site needs page links, so a menu button joins the logo
  plate and the phone pill. The service line remains the home page's own
  navigation. Grammar unchanged; this is the same build, not a new fingerprint.
- **Site:** residential, commercial, emergency, about, questions, booking,
  thanks and 404 pages, written from the facts on the company's own pages.
- **Backend:** zero-dependency Node server, stored requests, office inbox.
